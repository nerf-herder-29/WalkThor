WalkThor 1.91.4 keeps off the bottom screen for emulators that use both screens, can pause your home app while you play, and checks for and installs updates to your emulators and WalkThor itself. Changes since 1.87.1:

## New in 1.91.4
- **A game report after each game** (Diagnostics → Share game report). A couple of seconds after a game closes, WalkThor saves the device's own records of how it ended. That covers Android's record of the game's app (crash, native crash, memory killer or closed on purpose), every app killed or started in the last few minutes and why, the memory killer's and the crash log's lines, the Linux kernel's log (its own out-of-memory killer and the graphics driver), memory figures, and WalkThor's memory log for the same minutes. It stays on the device until you share it, and the last three are kept. It needs AYN's system helper.
- **The memory log says why each game closed,** from Android's own record: the app crashed (and whether in its native code, which is usually an emulator's own fault), froze, closed itself, or was closed by the memory killer and by which rule, or closed on purpose. This also covers a game that ended while WalkThor was restarting.
- **WalkThor keeps lean during games, always.** While a game plays, WalkThor's app is closed if it's only sitting in the background, since the drawer has everything it does. When you put the drawer away, it's let go straight away, along with cached images and rendered guide pages. Reopening it takes a moment longer, and it comes back where you were: the same tab, the same guide, the same page.
- **The loading log.** While a game loads, the black bottom screen shows a dim line of free memory every 6 seconds, so you can watch it drop and then "steadying" as it levels off, with "Tap for WalkThor" at the bottom. It's drawn straight on the black window, so it adds next to nothing to memory.
- **WalkThor comes up once the game has loaded.** With "Open when an emulator starts" on, WalkThor used to come up the moment a game started. It sat on the bottom screen while the game loaded, which is the game's hungriest minute, and that's when games were being closed for memory. Now the bottom screen stays plain black while the game loads (next to no memory), and WalkThor zooms in once memory has stopped dropping: at least 10 seconds in, no big fall for 8 seconds, no critical warning for 8, and low memory pressure. That's usually 12–20 seconds, and never more than 2½ minutes. Tap the black screen to bring WalkThor up straight away. The memory log notes the wait and when WalkThor came up.

- **Both screens together moves the volume too.** The bottom screen has a volume of its own, separate from the top screen's. WalkThor's volume slider and shortcuts only moved the top screen's, so the two drifted apart. With "Both screens together" on, the bottom screen's volume now follows: from the slider, from the volume shortcuts, and when you turn the switch on. Diagnostics also lists the device's volume settings.

- **Both screens, for dual-screen emulators** (Settings → Emulators → the emulator list). Turn on Both screens for an emulator that draws on the bottom screen too, and WalkThor stays off that screen while it plays. It doesn't open there with "Open when an emulator starts", doesn't black it out with "Turn bottom screen off during games", and doesn't put its cover over it when memory runs short. It still counts as a game for everything else: play time, memory protection, pausing your home app, and the performance overlay. Azahar, Citra and its forks (Lime3DS, Borked3DS, Mandarine), melonDS and DraStic are set to Both screens already; switch any of them off, or others on.

## New in 1.89.13
- **The drawer zooms in when it comes up by itself:** when a game starts (with "Open when an emulator starts" on), and when it comes back mid-game after stepping aside for memory or after Android closed it. The bottom screen goes black the instant it's called up, and WalkThor grows in out of the black, from slightly smaller, so what was on the bottom screen never shows through. The full app does the same when it's the one that opens. Opening the drawer yourself still slides it up, and putting it away still slides it down.

## New
- **Updates.** There's a new Updates item in the library's left-hand list, and the same page is one swipe left of Home in the drawer when no game is running. It shows WalkThor and each app you've marked as an emulator as cards: the version you have, the newest release, and an Update button when there's a newer one. Tap Update to download it, see its progress on the card, and hand it to Android's installer, which asks you before installing. Tap a card for what's new in that release, its release page, or to change where WalkThor looks.
  - WalkThor already knows where several emulators publish their releases. For any other emulator, tap **Set release page** and paste its GitHub link, or a Forgejo or Gitea link.
  - Before installing, WalkThor checks that the download is the same app, signed by the same developer as the one you have. If it isn't (a fork, say), the card tells you so, because Android would refuse to install it over yours.
  - **Works offline.** Without the internet, Updates shows what it last found and when, and checks again once you're back online. It checks on its own at most every 6 hours when you open it, or whenever you tap Check now.
  - The list item shows how many updates are waiting. The left-hand list now appears even if you haven't linked a ROMs folder, showing Guides and Updates.
  - The first time you update, Android asks you to allow WalkThor to install apps.

## Fixed in 1.91.4
- **WalkThor coming up for a game no longer sets off a burst of memory use.** Since 1.89.12, WalkThor let go of its app icons when a game started. When the drawer came up, Home's dock then reloaded the icon of every app on the device at once, and Android's critical warning often came the same second. Some games didn't survive it. The icons are now kept (about 8 MB). If they do need loading during a game, the dock loads only your pinned apps.
- **The loading wait also checks Android's memory pressure,** meaning how much time apps are spending waiting on memory. WalkThor comes up only once that's low too, not just once free memory has stopped dropping.
- **The drawer comes back where you were after it's been let go mid-game.** It used to open on Home again. Now it reopens on the same tab and guide, for as long as you're in the same game.
- **WalkThor stays calm during games.** Android's "most serious" memory warning came even with 7 GB free. Android gives it by how few apps are idling in the background, not by how much memory is left. WalkThor treated every one as an emergency. It took its drawer off the bottom screen, then rebuilt and brought it back a few seconds later, and games were closing just as it came back. Now:
  - **The warning only counts when memory really is short** (under 2.5 GB free). Otherwise the log notes it as ignored, with how much was free.
  - **WalkThor still comes back by itself mid-game, but only after real pressure.** It only steps off when memory really is short, then comes back once memory settles. If Android closes WalkThor during a game, the bottom screen stays black, not your home app, until memory settles and WalkThor comes back. Double-tap the black screen to bring it back sooner.
- **Your home app comes back even if AYN's helper is slow to answer after WalkThor restarts.** It's tried again every 5 seconds for up to 2 minutes, and the memory log says if it couldn't.
- **Your home app comes back if the game closes while WalkThor is restarting.** If Android closed WalkThor and the game around the same moment, the restarted WalkThor still believed the game was playing: your home app stayed paused, and the game's end wasn't logged. A couple of seconds after it starts, WalkThor now checks that the game it remembers is really running. If it isn't, everything that follows a game closing happens, and your home app comes back up on the screens.
- **Android's launcher is protected again while it stands in for your paused home app.** Leaving it unprotected (from 1.89.12) didn't free anything: Android starts whatever is home again the moment it's closed, so it only took the memory straight back, at the worst moment.
- **Both screens' brightness stays together.** With "Both screens together" on, the bottom screen could stop following the top during a game. After a critical memory warning, WalkThor held back changes to the bottom screen's brightness (it starts a small program through AYN's helper) until memory fully recovered, which in a heavy game could be the whole session, and dropped them instead of applying them later. Now it holds back only for the 10 seconds after a warning, then applies the newest level. It also keeps a waiting change for each screen, so one screen's change can't be lost when both are set at once.
- **The stick-light colour on screen matches the lights.** After you'd picked a colour in WalkThor, changing the lights from AYN's side (its settings or quick panel) changed the lights but not WalkThor's accents. WalkThor now notices when AYN's light settings change from what it set, and follows them. It checks at most every 5 seconds, so it costs nothing noticeable during games.
- **The Emulators list shows your apps again.** In 1.90.0 its longer explanation could push the search box and the list off a short screen, so only the explanation showed. The list now always keeps room. It also loads your apps again if they were let go to save memory while it was open.

## Fixed in 1.89.13
- **The time and battery stay where they are when the screen dims, during games too.** During a game the dim screen put them in the middle. On Home, which has no small time-and-battery readout, Home's own big clock and battery ring now stay bright right where they are, with everything else dimmed. On the other pages, the bright copy sits exactly over their own readout. Also, when WalkThor had zoomed in by itself (as a game started, or coming back mid-game), the dim screen drew its bright copy of the time and battery where they'd been partway through the zoom: smaller, and shifted toward the middle. It now checks where they actually are as the screen dims.
- **WalkThor uses less memory during games:**
  - **Fewer commands through AYN's system helper.** Each one starts a shell of its own. WalkThor checked each bottom-screen app with its own command every 3 seconds; now it's one command for all of them. Renewing their memory protection now happens every 6 seconds instead of 3, and Keeper's every 3 seconds instead of every second. Altogether that's about a third as many commands while you play.
  - **A smaller image cache, wherever WalkThor starts.** When only the drawer had run (no app), images used the image library's default cache, several times the size. Now the drawer and the app share a small one. Covers without see-through parts take half the memory.
- **Less memory taken from heavy games while the drawer is up.** Any screen with a brightness slider started a small helper program through AYN's system helper as soon as it appeared, so that dragging would be smooth. That program is a second Java runtime of its own, tens of MB that WalkThor's memory figure doesn't show. With "Open when an emulator starts" on, it started just as the game was loading hardest. WalkThor also started a one-off copy just to read the bottom screen's brightness. Now, while a game is playing, the program only starts when you actually drag a brightness slider, and the slider shows the last level WalkThor knew rather than asking. While memory is critical, nothing new is started at all. Outside games it's as before.
- **Games no longer crash when WalkThor opens with them.** With "Open when an emulator starts" on, WalkThor sat on the bottom screen while the game loaded. When memory ran critical, Android closed WalkThor, and WalkThor came back within 10 seconds whether or not there was memory for it. Coming back was what pushed the game over. Two changes:
  - **At the critical moment, WalkThor steps aside on its own.** It puts away its drawer (or app) on the bottom screen and leaves the plain black cover in its place. That takes almost no memory and stops the home app coming up underneath. Double-tap the black screen to bring the drawer back (or the app, if that's what was open). Otherwise the screen comes back on when the game closes. The memory log notes each step.
  - **Mid-game, WalkThor only comes back once memory has really recovered** (2.5 GB free). If it doesn't recover within a minute, the bottom screen stays black until the game is over.

## Fixed in 1.89.4
- **The bottom screen goes dark sooner when a game starts** (Turn bottom screen off during games). It waited 1.2 seconds after the top screen changed, starting the wait again each time a splash screen came up, so it could take a couple of seconds. Now it goes black a quarter of a second after the game appears. Coming back on still waits 1.2 seconds, so the screen doesn't light up while a game is still starting.

## Fixed in 1.89.3
- **Your home app comes back right away.** At first it waited 20 seconds after the game closed, sometimes 40. Now it's back as soon as the game is gone. Opening the drawer mid-game still doesn't count as leaving the game.
- **Close all apps brings your home app back, and says so:** "Closed all apps · Cocoon is back".

## Fixed in 1.89.1
- **Your home app comes back after the game.** WalkThor didn't notice Android's own launcher coming to the front (it has no app icon), so it thought the game was still playing and never put your home app back. It notices home apps now.

## New in 1.89.0
- **Pause your home app while you play** (Settings → Emulators → While you play). Android keeps whatever app is set as home running, and starts it again every time it's closed. A frontend like Cocoon restarting over and over can push a game out of memory: the bottom screen flashing white, then the game crashing. With this on, when a game starts WalkThor makes Android's own launcher home and closes your home app. As soon as you leave the game, your home app is home again and takes the launcher's place on screen. It works with any home app, not just Cocoon. If WalkThor stops mid-game, your home app comes back the next time WalkThor starts. Each switch is noted in the memory log. Needs AYN's system helper (Force SELinux off), and it's off until you turn it on.

## New in 1.88.3
- **Dolphin on the Updates page.** Dolphin doesn't put its releases on GitHub, so WalkThor now checks Dolphin's own releases and downloads the Android app from Dolphin's site. It understands Dolphin's version names (2603a, 2609a, and test builds like 2603a-514). If your Dolphin came from the Play Store, the card shows **Update in Play Store** instead, because only the Play Store can update its copy.

## Fixed in 1.88.2
- **Tapping a card on the drawer's Updates page works.** It used to crash or do nothing, because the drawer can't open pop-up windows. Release notes and the release-page box now open as a panel on the page itself, in the drawer and in WalkThor.
- **Release notes get the room they need.** They used to be squeezed into a narrow box. Now they fill the page, with Release page, Change page and Update along the bottom.
- **The drawer shows that Updates is there.** A small "Updates ›" tab on Home's right edge shows how many updates are waiting, and a "‹ Home" tab leads back. Swipe, or tap a tab.

## Fixed in 1.88.1
- **Updates no longer says you're offline when you aren't.** It asked Android whether there was a connection, which WalkThor isn't allowed to ask, so the answer was always no. Now it just tries the release pages and only shows the offline banner if none of them answer. One emulator's site being down no longer counts as offline either: that card says its site didn't answer.

## Changed
- **Two-finger scrolling on the trackpad follows your fingers live.** Before, it sent a short separate swipe after every bit of movement, so pages crawled. Now it's one continuous touch on the top screen that moves with your fingers, a little over twice as far as they go. Lift your fingers mid-flick and the page keeps scrolling; when the scroll reaches the edge of the top screen, it carries on from the pointer.

## Fixed
- **The performance overlay no longer shows over your home app.** WalkThor only counted the launcher set as Android's default home as "not a game", so a home app on the top screen that isn't that one (a frontend, say) was taken for a game. Now any app that can be a home screen is never counted as a game, unless you've marked it as an emulator yourself. The same fix applies everywhere WalkThor watches for a game: memory protection, play time and the drawer.

## Privacy
- Updates contacts only the release pages (GitHub, or the Forgejo or Gitea site you gave it), and only when you open Updates or tap Check now. Nothing about you or your apps is sent. The privacy policy explains this.

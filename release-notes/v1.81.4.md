WalkThor 1.81.4 brings together everything since 1.72.0: a simpler WalkThor that works with any frontend, button shortcuts, its own keyboard, an FPS counter, closing all apps at once, and more reliable comebacks.

## Fixed in 1.81.4: closing all apps
- **Close all apps works as it did in 1.81.1 again.** 1.81.2 and 1.81.3 changed how WalkThor read the system helper's answers, which made closing all apps slow and left apps running. That change is undone.
- **It just says "Closed all apps"**, not a list of every app. Which ones closed is still in About & licences → Diagnostics.
- **Fewer false "Couldn't close" messages.** A harmless note from the cleanup, sent when an app had already closed, no longer counts as that app still running.

## Fixed in 1.81.1: keeping the bottom screen's apps alive
- **The protection from 1.80.4 now actually happens.** AYN's system helper only passes back the first line of what a command prints, so WalkThor never saw whether it had ranked your home app and itself just below the game, and the close records never came through. It now reads every line, and WalkThor protects itself the moment it starts, not a few seconds later, since a freshly restarted WalkThor is the easiest thing for the memory killer to take again.
- **No flicker from WalkThor during a squeeze.** If Android closed WalkThor for memory in the last minute, the FPS counter waits 10 seconds before coming back, instead of being put straight back and taken again.
- **The memory log says more:** how much swap is in use, how the home app and WalkThor rank for the memory killer, the killer's own settings, and its record of each close with the rule it used.

## New in 1.81.0: lock the controller to a screen
- **Two new shortcut actions: Lock controller to top screen, and Lock controller to bottom screen.** Android sends button presses to whichever screen you touched last, so tapping the bottom screen mid-game takes the controller away from your game. Locked to a screen, the controller stays there: whenever Android moves it to the other screen, WalkThor moves it straight back. Press the same shortcut again to unlock. A message says which, and the lock stays on after WalkThor restarts.
- With Force SELinux off it moves the controller back without touching anything; otherwise with one short tap on the very top edge of the locked screen. Typing in WalkThor's own search still works while locked to the top screen.

## 1.80.4
- **The bottom screen's apps hold on longer in heavy games.** While a game is in front, WalkThor ranks your home app (whichever you use) and itself just below the game for Android's memory killer, so they're closed only after everything else, and the game always stays safest. Needs Force SELinux off. Tip: if you have swap (virtual memory) turned on, try turning it off. With swap on, Android can start closing apps while gigabytes of memory are still free.
- **The memory log tells the whole story.** Each time WalkThor starts, and as games end, the log adds why Android last closed your home app and WalkThor, and the memory killer's own record of each close, so one shared log shows what happened even when WalkThor itself was closed.
- **Close all apps is quicker:** the ring fills over one second, with no number in it.

## Memory during games (1.80.3)
- **A memory log, for the bottom screen flashing in heavy games.** While a game is in front, WalkThor notes the free memory every few seconds, and, with Force SELinux off, whenever Android closes your home app, the app on the bottom screen or WalkThor and it starts again, with Android's reason. It's in About & licences → Diagnostics → Memory during games: play until the bottom screen flashes, then tap Share log.
- **WalkThor is leaner while you play.** When a game comes up it lets go of cached guide pages and images. The drawer is fully taken down once it's been put away for 30 seconds during a game, and straight away if Android runs short of memory, so the game and your home app have more room. It's built again the next time you open it, which can take a moment longer.

## WalkThor on its own
- **Works with any frontend.** WalkThor no longer reads Cocoon's or ES-DE's logs. It only looks at whether one of your emulators is in front (Settings → Emulators), so games started from a frontend, a shortcut or the home screen all work the same. BananaNative and other shortcut apps count once ticked in Choose emulators.
- **Home while you play.** During a game, the drawer shows the same Home as the rest of the time, with the tabs along the top: Multi, Guide, Mirror, Controls, Stats, Shots, Captions, Light & Sound and Apps. Home keeps its usual proportions under the tabs. Only Home dims; the tabs and the time stay lit.
- **Guide asks which guide.** The Guide tab lists your guides each time, newest first.
- **Your games, by system.** Opening WalkThor shows the games in your ROMs folder under each system, each with its guide beside it. Tap *Add a guide* to pick one of your guides or add a file; press and hold a guide to change it or take it off. Nintendo Switch 2 is recognised (a `switch2`, `ns2`, `nsw2` or `nintendoswitch2` folder).
- **Pinned tab and play time are for every game.** Press and hold a tab to have the drawer open on it whatever you're playing. Play time is a daily total.
- **Simpler settings:** General, Shortcuts, Screen, Emulators, Guides and About.
- **Removed:** the Now playing screen, cover art and game names, the artwork folder, artwork scraping, and the Cocoon, ES-DE and Artwork settings. WalkThor no longer goes online except to download the Captions speech model, if you ask it to.

## Shortcuts
- **Settings → Shortcuts** sets what each of these does: the AYN button's press, double press and hold, and the Back button pressed twice or held. (New in 1.80.1: holding Back takes the place of holding Start + Select, and carries over whatever you'd set for it. Fixed in 1.80.2: pressing Back twice no longer also counts as holding it.)
- **What they can do:** open the drawer, AYN's own drawer, dim the bottom screen, turn it off, close all apps, take a screenshot, save the last seconds of play, the FPS counter on or off, the stick lights on or off, locking the controller to the top or bottom screen, and the next performance or fan mode.
- **Each one rumbles and says what it did** in a short message on screen ("Drawer open", "Bottom screen dimmed", "Screenshot saved"…). The rumble can be switched off under Shortcuts → Feedback.
- Your earlier AYN button, hold and Back-twice settings carry over.

## Close all apps
- **One press closes everything you have open**, the game included, from a shortcut or the X on Home's dock. Your home app and WalkThor stay. It closes what's in front on each screen and everything running or in your recent apps, checks each one really stopped, and clears their cards from recent apps.
- **A countdown first.** The top screen goes black with a warning that your game closes too and anything unsaved is lost, with a line in your stick-light colour filling along its bottom edge. The bottom screen's ring fills over one second in the same colour. Tap either screen, or press Back, B or Select, to cancel.
- A message says what was closed. About → Diagnostics has the detail.

## WalkThor keyboard
- **Its own keyboard, in place of AYN's** (Settings → General). It fills the whole bottom screen, every time, with a row of numbers, three rows of letters and a bottom row, on glass keys. The action key takes your stick lights' colour and says what it does (Go, Search, Next, Done…).
- Tap shift for one capital, twice for caps lock. Hold backspace to keep deleting. "#+=" switches to symbols; hold it, or press Back, to put the keyboard away. Hold space to pick another keyboard.
- What you type goes only to the app you're typing in; WalkThor doesn't keep or send any of it.

## FPS counter
- **The top screen's frame rate in a small smoked-glass pill** (Settings → Screen), on the bottom screen so it never covers your game, or on the top screen if you prefer, in any corner. It's read from the display driver once a second and hides while the bottom screen is off. It comes back after WalkThor restarts.

## Bottom screen
- **Bottom screen off during games** (Settings → Screen): it turns off when a game comes up and back on when you leave it. It only covers the screen in black, so it can never get stuck off if WalkThor stops.
- **Dim the bottom screen** right down from a shortcut, and back up to the top screen's brightness the same way.

## Stick lights
- **Stick lights on or off** in Settings → General, or from a shortcut. Turning them back on brings back the colour they had.

## Coming back after Android closes WalkThor
- **Faster and more reliable.** A small keeper process notices straight away when Android closes WalkThor and starts it again, with a standing alarm behind it. The drawer comes back if it was up, within 10 seconds at most.
- **No flicking back and forth.** If Android closes WalkThor again within 3 minutes of it coming back, it stays down until you open it. It never opens the full app in the drawer's place.
- The setting is now called "Bring WalkThor back".

## Look
- **One font everywhere:** the keyboard, the FPS counter, messages and the close-all countdown use Space Grotesk, like the rest of WalkThor.
- Home's battery ring sits tidily, with no gap between the percentage and the word under it.
